FAQ

Isn't NOAA weather data free?

Yes, and this tool is built on it. What you're paying for is the processing: 30 winters of daily records turned into storm and push counts at your trigger for 1,445 stations, the percentile pricing, the loss backtest and a client-ready report, in seconds instead of hours in a spreadsheet.

The nearest station isn't at my property. Is it still useful?

Use it as the local baseline. The ZIP search shows the 5 nearest stations with distance and elevation, so you can pick the one with similar terrain and lake exposure. Snowfall varies locally, especially in lake-effect and mountain areas, so adjust with your own experience.

How do you count "storms" and "pushes"?

A storm is a run of consecutive days with at least 0.1" of new snow, summed. It counts if the total reaches your trigger. Pushes = 1 per qualifying storm, plus 1 for each "extra push" increment you set (for example, every 6" beyond the trigger). Stations measure snowfall once a day, so storm timing can split or merge events.

Does it handle ice and freezing rain?

No. Snowfall records don't capture ice storms, freezing rain or refreeze. Salting counts use snow days (≥0.1") as a proxy. Add ice events from your own experience.

Will this guarantee I make money on seasonal contracts?

No. It shows how a price would have performed in past winters. Future winters can be lighter or heavier than anything on record. It's a planning input, not a forecast or financial advice.

Which areas are covered?

1,445 NOAA stations in 43 states with at least 24 complete winters of snowfall records and a median of 5"+ per winter. Coverage is strongest in the Midwest, Northeast, Great Lakes, Plains and Mountain West. Try the demo or the free table first.
